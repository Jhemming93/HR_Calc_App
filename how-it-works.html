How the calculator works

Highrise Gold Lab turns the prices and probabilities you enter into a clearer estimate of profit, break-even points, and expected return. It does not pull live market data, so the quality of the result depends on the prices and odds you provide.

Flip an item

Enter what you paid, the price you think the item can realistically sell for, and how you plan to sell it. The calculator estimates what you receive after the selected fee and compares that with your cost.

Grab odds

Enter the cost of a spin, the displayed chance of each outcome, and a realistic resale value. The calculator weights each outcome by its probability to estimate the average return over many spins.

Shop purchase

Compare a fixed shop price with a possible later resale price. This can help you see the minimum resale value needed to break even or hit a target profit.

Local saving

Your settings can be saved in your browser using localStorage. They are not sent to a Highrise Gold Lab account or server.

Core calculations

ResultMeaning
Net proceedsSale price minus the seller-side fee selected in the calculator.
ProfitNet proceeds minus your acquisition cost.
Break-even sale priceThe minimum sale price that would approximately recover your cost after the selected fee.
Maximum buy for target profitThe most you could pay while still reaching the target profit at the entered resale price.
Expected valueThe probability-weighted average result across all entered Grab outcomes.
Important: expected value is an average, not a promise. A single pull can be much better or worse than the calculated average.

Why realistic sale prices matter

An asking price is not necessarily a market value. For better estimates, use prices that items are actually likely to sell for, account for how quickly you need to sell, and revisit your assumptions when supply or demand changes.

What the calculator intentionally does not assume

The tool does not automatically decide whether a rarity is valuable, whether a listed item will sell, or whether every Grab distributes probability equally among items of the same rarity. Those assumptions can be wrong, so the calculator keeps them visible and editable.
