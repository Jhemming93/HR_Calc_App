Grab odds & expected value

The Grab calculator is designed to answer a specific question: based on the probabilities and resale values you enter, what is the average economic return of a spin?

Use the odds shown on the specific Grab

Do not assume that every Grab uses the same chance for Common, Rare, Epic, Legendary, or Mythic items. Probability can vary by Grab. Use the information displayed for the Grab you are evaluating.

Rarity and probability are different inputs

Rarity is a label. Probability is the actual chance of receiving an outcome. The calculator keeps both visible because the same rarity can appear at different rates in different Grabs.

How expected value works

If an outcome has a 10% chance and a realistic net value of 1,000g, it contributes an average of 100g to the expected return. The calculator repeats that weighting for every outcome, adds the results, and compares the total with the cost of the spin.

Example: a positive expected value does not mean your next spin is profitable. It means the probability-weighted average is positive across a sufficiently large number of comparable spins under the same assumptions.

Entering a Grab accurately

  1. Enter the Gold cost of one spin.
  2. Add every possible outcome or a carefully constructed group of outcomes.
  3. Select the rarity for readability.
  4. Enter the probability shown by Highrise.
  5. Enter a realistic resale price rather than an optimistic listing.
  6. Confirm that the probabilities total 100%.

When grouping outcomes

If several items truly have equal probability and similar resale values, grouping can simplify the calculation. If their individual odds or market values differ materially, enter them separately. Do not automatically divide a rarity's total probability equally unless the Grab information supports that assumption.

Pity, guarantees, and special mechanics

Some Grab systems can include guarantees or other mechanics that make repeated pulls behave differently from a simple independent-spin model. When such mechanics apply, a basic expected-value calculation may not capture the full sequence. Treat the calculator as a baseline unless the exact mechanic has been modeled explicitly.

What to do with an item you already pulled

Once you already own a specific item, its future resale decision is no longer mainly about the original Grab probability. Use the Flip calculator to compare your chosen cost basis with today's likely resale price and fee.
